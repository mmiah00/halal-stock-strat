Reading this responsibly

  • Look-ahead / survivorship bias: the halal universe is today's candidate list projected backward. Stocks added or delisted over time aren't reconstructed point-in-time.
  • Price-only: IBKR TRADES bars are split-adjusted but not dividend-adjusted.
  • No transaction costs: commissions, spread, and market impact from monthly rebalancing are excluded — this matters most for the relative strength rotation, which reshuffles roughly half its 10-name roster every month (see its turnover row in the stats table).
  • Cash earns 0%: the trend-filter strategy's unfilled slots contribute nothing, not a risk-free rate.
  • Volatility as a denominator is unstable near zero: the volatility-adjusted momentum strategy can hand an outsized score to a stock purely because its recent 60-day window happened to be unusually quiet, independent of how strong its actual return was.
  • The breakout system trades continuously, not monthly: unlike the other five, it can enter and exit positions on any trading day, so its stats here are computed from its daily NAV curve resampled to month-end for comparability — that resampling actually understates its risk: the notebook's own daily-frequency figures show a deeper max drawdown (about -25%) than the monthly-sampled -20% shown here. Its stops and channel exits are also assumed to fill at exactly their trigger price, with no slippage or overnight gap risk, which flatters a fast-reacting system like this one more than a monthly-rebalanced one.
  • RSI pullback trades continuously too, and sells into strength on purpose: same daily-vs-monthly stats caveat as the breakout system above. Its very high win rate (see its own notebook) comes from exiting once RSI recovers to 60 or a name is up 10% — it structurally gives up the large winners a trend-following system is built to capture, so a high hit rate here is not the same claim as the best return.
  • Z-score reversion has no protective stop at all, and it shows: its monthly-sampled max drawdown here understates the real risk even more than the breakout or RSI systems do — the notebook's own daily-frequency max drawdown runs deeper than what the monthly resampling shows on this page. A single name (its own notebook found NVDA once down 26% on one trade) can keep falling well past the -2 Z-score entry threshold with nothing to cut the loss short.
  • Halal status: every ticker here is a candidate only — verify current Shariah compliance independently before investing.
